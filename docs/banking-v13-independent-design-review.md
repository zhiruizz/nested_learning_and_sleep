# Banking V13 draft: independent design review

## Disposition

The proposed 2×2 Sleep factorial is a useful, bounded follow-up to V12: it puts the paper-listed effective batch 32 into the full local on-policy GKD+LTI pipeline and includes the V12-motivated batch-1/high-rate comparison. The explicit limits against a paper-reproduction claim, the shared 32-example wake policy across arms, and the CMS reference-only designation are sound.

The scheduler does not have the apparent level-1 collision when checked against the actual inherited receipts. V10 foundation wake cursors are 348, 326 and 322 for seeds 17, 29 and 43. The V12 first due receipts then record ticks 352, 336 and 336, each with `due_levels: [0]`; the level-1 window is not due at those boundaries. Under the V13 schedule this yields the stated first sender-0 event, a second sender-0 event 16 ticks later at 368, 352 and 352, then 15 post-event wake ticks ending at 383, 367 and 367, before the next level-0 due point. The original claim that a level-1 Sleep transaction must be added was incorrect and is withdrawn. Keep the V10 behavior of processing all actually due levels, and have the V13 CPU clock preflight assert these receipt-derived cursors, both level-0 due events, no other due level, and the final cursor for all seeds before any GPU run.

## Fairness and estimands

For the four primary Sleep arms, the 960-request/event comparison is request-exposure matched: batch 1 makes 960 optimizer updates per event, while batch 32 makes 30. It therefore estimates the combined effect of aggregation and fewer updates at fixed prompt/request exposure; it is not a batch-size-only effect. The within-batch LR contrasts are better isolated because exposure and effective update counts match within each batch size. `B32_LOW_DOSE` is appropriately named as a dose contrast: 3,200 requests produce 100 updates/event, so it changes exposure and update count together. Keep it outside the pure batch/LR interpretation.

`CMS_ONLY` uses the same wake batch size and scheduled wake steps but omits Sleep work. The config correctly says it is not compute- or information-matched. It can contextualize the lifecycle endpoints, but a performance difference against CMS alone cannot be attributed to Sleep independent of added compute, generated training targets, and receiver capacity. Keep the causal primary comparisons among Sleep arms, report per-arm requests, actual generated tokens, backbone forwards, backward calls, optimizer updates and wall time, and state that CMS is a lower-cost reference. A compute-matched supervised-wake control would answer a different question and should not be silently introduced into this frozen grid.

The event selector uses a common deterministic request schedule with a 3,200-position stride, which is sensible for matching prompt identities across the 960- and 3,200-request conditions. Key selector state and counters by the two actual level-0 boundary events and verify that event 2 starts at the predeclared schedule offset rather than reusing event 1 requests. Since GKD is on-policy, matching prompts does not make generated targets identical across arms; log owner, generated-token counts and role composition per boundary. That difference is part of the treatment, not a violation of prompt matching.

## V12 evidence and optimizer claims

V12 supplies a reasonable hypothesis, not a transferable result. At 960 fixed-teacher request exposures, batch-32/high-rate had lower fixed-trajectory fit KL than batch-1/high-rate (0.0490 vs 0.0957), but its global-77 development accuracy was lower (52.27% vs 53.79%). The low-rate batch-32 condition also remained far from the teacher fit after its 3,200-request dose. These were fixed-teacher KL-only receiver fits at one boundary, not full on-policy GKD+LTI training. V13 should therefore report the Table-5-like 5e-6/batch-32 arm as a tested local configuration, not as a paper-reproduced recipe or expected winner; the 1e-3 arm is an explicit local comparison informed by prior development data.

The paper audit supports only a structural and settings-level comparison: Table 5 lists effective batch 32, LR 5e-6 and 100 Sleep steps as general settings, but does not specify the class-incremental task's separate wake/replay batch, per-boundary sample count, optimizer-step mapping, task order, or exact clock mapping. V13's minibatch clock, local additive CMS experts, fixed KL/reward proxy coefficients and two-boundary schedule remain implementation choices. Use the protocol's qualification that this is not native HOPE/Sleep fidelity or full-paper reproduction.

## Seeds, endpoints and budget checks before freeze

Three class-order seeds sharing the same foundation lineage are appropriate for a bounded development comparison, but they are not three independent pretrained-model replications. Preserve all seeds and all five predeclared development stages; do not choose a checkpoint, arm, or LR based on those scores. Keep old/current/all-77 denominators fixed at 110/110/220 for each seed and stage, and report both all-label teacher-forced ranking and unconstrained greedy separately.

Interpret the configured 14,400-second, 200,000-forward and 8,000-update limits as per-run ceilings, consistent with the existing runner's per-run budget accounting. Check each of the six arms in each of the three seeds independently against those ceilings; report study totals as sums across completed runs, without treating them as a single shared cap. Include candidate scoring, on-policy teacher/student generation and LTI calls in each run's actual work. Count inherited task-0 work as historical common initialization, and task-1 wake batch 32 as part of every arm; do not add the V12 32:64 restore qualification into V13's scientific arm budget.

No official test or new dataset is needed for this development study. Freeze the event-level schedule, due-level semantics, limits, and source/config hashes before inspecting V13 development scores. Retain all engineering failures/costs separately from successful arm counters.
